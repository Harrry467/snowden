A weekend of hiking, camping & good company

Three days at Yr Wyddfa

Camping at Llyn Gwynant, with a summit day, good food and plenty of time to enjoy the mountains.

  1. 1
    Sunday 25 October

    Arrive & set up camp

    Aim to reach Llyn Gwynant with enough daylight to pitch tents and settle in. The clocks go back that night, so we'll have an earlier sunset than we're used to. Then it's a relaxed first evening: food at camp, or a short walk to the Pen y Gwryd Hotel if anyone fancies a pub.

  2. 2
    Monday 26 October

    Summit day

    The big day. Start early — see the route options below — and leave ourselves plenty of time to enjoy the walk without rushing. With ten people, we'll naturally move at a group pace. Back at camp for a hot meal and a well-earned rest.

  3. 3
    Tuesday 27 October

    Pack down & head home

    A shorter day: strike camp, do a last check for litter and gear left behind, and head off. If legs allow, Beddgelert or Llyn Dinas make for a gentle, flat stroll before the drive home.

Getting to the top: three route options

All six paths up Yr Wyddfa are officially graded Hard or Strenuous by Eryri National Park. They're proper mountain days, but with a sensible route choice and good preparation, there's plenty to look forward to. With ten of us, it's worth agreeing on one route as a group before setting off.

Closest to camp

Watkin Path

Starts
Pont Bethania car park, a short drive or walk from Llyn Gwynant
Distance
~8 miles round trip
Ascent
~1,015m
Time
6–7 hours

A beautiful, varied route with some steep and rocky sections, particularly higher up. It's convenient from camp, which makes the logistics nice and simple.

Shortest

Miners' / Pyg Track

Starts
Pen y Pass (needs pre-booked parking, or the Sherpa'r Wyddfa bus)
Distance
~7 miles round trip
Ascent
~700–740m
Time
5–6 hours

A very popular choice with a small car park, so plan parking or the bus ahead of time. It's shorter than the Watkin Path, with some steep sections and great mountain scenery.

Gentlest gradient

Llanberis Path

Starts
Llanberis village, ~25 minutes' drive away
Distance
~9 miles round trip
Ascent
~950m
Time
5–7 hours

The longest option, but with a gentler gradient for much of the way and a straightforward line to follow. A good option if the group wants a steadier pace.

About the railway: The railway is worth checking before the trip, as its seasonal timetable can change. Hafod Eryri (the summit café and toilets) is scheduled to close for the season on 25 October, so for our summit day on the 26th we'll plan to be self-sufficient and check the latest railway information before travelling.

Read this before summit day

Make the most of the mountain

Late October can bring crisp, changeable mountain weather, so a little preparation helps us make the most of the day. The aim is simple: enjoy the walk, look after each other and have a great weekend.

~9.5
roughly how many hours of daylight you'll have on the 26th and 27th. The clocks go back on arrival day, so we'll have an earlier sunset — another good reason to make an early start on summit day.

Start walking early, agree a turnaround time before you set off, and stick to it even if the summit is close. Everyone should carry a head torch, even for a day hike — if anyone's slow, or the weather turns, you don't want to be finding it out in the dark.

Weather & ground conditions

Summit facilities: Hafod Eryri (the summit café and toilets) closes for the season on 25 October. For the 26 October hike, use the toilet before setting off and carry enough food and water for the full day.

The summit is usually colder, wetter and windier than the valley floor at Llyn Gwynant — the railway’s guide says summit temperatures are frequently around 8–10°C colder than the valley, before wind chill. Bring layers so you're comfortable if conditions change. Check a proper mountain forecast (not just a general weather app) the night before and again on the morning, for example the Mountain Weather Information Service at mwis.org.uk.

Late October can bring frost, ice or occasionally snow on the higher sections. Check Eryri National Park's ground condition reports beforehand, but note that this service is seasonal and may not yet be running in late October. If conditions become more wintry than the group is equipped or experienced for, that's a good point to turn around and save the summit for another day.

Navigation

Phone signal can be patchy on the mountain, so it's smart to have a paper map (OS Explorer OL17 covers the whole area) and a compass with the group. Make sure more than one person is comfortable using them, while keeping phones charged as a useful extra.

Making a group of ten work

  • If natural pace differences emerge, consider two small groups, each with someone confident on navigation, with a clear plan for regrouping.
  • Agree a meeting point and time to regroup — at the summit, and back at the cars or camp.
  • Do a quick headcount at the top and again once everyone's back down.
  • Stick together where practical, and agree a simple plan if people naturally spread out.
  • Leave your planned route and expected return time with someone staying at camp, or a friend or family member back home.

In camp

Llyn Gwynant sits in a sheltered valley, but late-October evenings can still feel chilly. Peg tents down well for comfort in wind and rain, and keep a dry set of clothes for a cosy evening around camp.

If we need help

Call 999 or 112, and ask for Police, then Mountain Rescue. Give your location as precisely as you can — a grid reference from your map, or a what3words address, is far more useful than "near the top."

Pack for a comfortable day

What to bring

Everything is split into shared camp gear and each person's summit-day pack. Tick things off as you go.

Camp & group gear

  • Weatherproof tent(s), guy lines staked out for wind
  • Warm sleeping bag & sleeping mat suitable for a chilly October night
  • Camp stove, fuel & cooking gear
  • Head torches + spare batteries for camp
  • Portable power bank(s)
  • Group first aid kit
  • Dry bags for spare clothes
  • Bin bags — pack out everything you bring in
  • Cash/card for the campsite
  • Wellies or camp shoes for around the site
  • OS Explorer OL17 map & compass

In your pack on summit day

  • Comfortable, worn-in walking boots
  • Waterproof jacket and trousers
  • Warm mid-layer (fleece or softshell — no cotton)
  • Hat and gloves
  • Enough water for the full walk (around 2 litres is a useful guide)
  • Packed lunch & high-energy snacks
  • Head torch & spare batteries, even for a day hike
  • Phone, fully charged, plus a power bank
  • Personal first aid basics & blister plasters
  • Whistle
  • Some cash
  • A small bin bag for your own rubbish
