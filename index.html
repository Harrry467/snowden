A group hike & camp for around 10

Three days at Yr Wyddfa

Camping at Llyn Gwynant, with a shot at the summit of Wales' highest mountain in between.

  1. 1
    Sunday 25 October

    Arrive & set up camp

    Aim to reach Llyn Gwynant with enough daylight to pitch tents and get bearings — this is the weekend the clocks go back, so the evening arrives an hour earlier than you'd expect. A relaxed first night: food at camp, or a short walk to the Pen y Gwryd Hotel if anyone fancies a pub; Beddgelert is roughly 5 miles away, so treat that as a drive rather than an evening walk.

  2. 2
    Monday 26 October

    Summit day

    The main event. Start early — see the route options below — and build in a buffer, since a group of ten will move at the pace of its slowest member. Back at camp by dark with a hot meal waiting is the goal.

  3. 3
    Tuesday 27 October

    Pack down & head home

    A shorter day: strike camp, do a last check for litter and gear left behind, and head off. If legs allow, Beddgelert or Llyn Dinas make for a gentle, flat stroll before the drive home.

Getting to the top: three route options

All six paths up Yr Wyddfa are officially graded Hard or Strenuous by Eryri National Park — none of them are a casual walk, whatever their reputation. With ten of you, it's worth agreeing on one route as a group rather than splitting up on the mountain.

Closest to camp

Watkin Path

Starts
Pont Bethania car park, a short drive or walk from Llyn Gwynant
Distance
~8 miles round trip
Ascent
~1,015m
Time
6–7 hours

Steep and rocky, especially near the top and on the knees on the way down. Convenient logistically, but don't pick it just for that reason.

Shortest

Miners' / Pyg Track

Starts
Pen y Pass (needs pre-booked parking, or the Sherpa'r Wyddfa bus)
Distance
~7 miles round trip
Ascent
~700–740m
Time
5–6 hours

Very popular, with a small car park that can fill early. Shorter than the Watkin Path, but still steep in places. The Llanberis Path is the most popular route overall.

Gentlest gradient

Llanberis Path

Starts
Llanberis village, ~25 minutes' drive away
Distance
~9 miles round trip
Ascent
~950m
Time
5–7 hours

Longest in distance but the least steep, following the railway line most of the way. A reasonable choice if fitness levels in the group vary a lot.

About the railway: the railway information is inconsistent: its site says the summit building (Hafod Eryri, including café and toilets) closes for the season on 25 October, while its timetable information has also listed diesel services beyond that date. For 26 October, assume there will be no summit café or toilets and do not rely on the railway for transport; check the operator’s latest timetable before travelling.

Read this before summit day

Mountains don't care about your itinerary

Late October on Yr Wyddfa is a different mountain to the one in the summer photos. A bit of planning is what keeps a big group day out fun instead of frightening.

~9.5
roughly how many hours of daylight you'll have on the 26th and 27th — and it gets dark fast once the sun's gone. The clocks go back the morning you arrive, so evenings close in an hour earlier than they did the week before.

Start walking early, agree a turnaround time before you set off, and stick to it even if the summit is close. Everyone should carry a head torch, even for a day hike — if anyone's slow, or the weather turns, you don't want to be finding it out in the dark.

Weather & ground conditions

Summit facilities: Hafod Eryri (the summit café and toilets) closes for the season on 25 October. For the 26 October hike, use the toilet before setting off and carry enough food and water for the full day.

The summit is routinely colder, wetter and windier than the valley floor at Llyn Gwynant — the railway’s guide says summit temperatures are frequently around 8–10°C colder than the valley, before wind chill. Check a proper mountain forecast (not just a general weather app) the night before and again on the morning, for example the Mountain Weather Information Service at mwis.org.uk.

Late October can bring the first frost, ice or even snow on the higher sections. Check Eryri National Park's ground condition reports beforehand, but note that this service is seasonal and may not yet be running in late October. If you meet ice or snow near the top and nobody has crampons, an ice axe, and experience using them, that's the turnaround point — not a reason to push on carefully.

Navigation

Phone signal is patchy to non-existent on parts of the mountain, and battery drains fast in the cold. Carry a paper map (OS Explorer OL17 covers the whole area) and a compass between you, and make sure more than one person in the group can actually use them — don't rely on a phone app as your only method of finding the way.

Managing a group of ten

  • Consider splitting into a faster and a slower group, each with someone confident on navigation, rather than trying to keep ten people moving at one pace.
  • Agree a meeting point and time to regroup — at the summit, and back at the cars or camp.
  • Do a headcount at the top and again once everyone's back down. It's easy to assume someone's just behind when they're not.
  • Nobody walks alone, and nobody goes on ahead out of sight of the group without agreeing where to wait.
  • Leave your planned route and expected return time with someone staying at camp, or a friend or family member back home.

In camp

Llyn Gwynant sits in a valley, so it stays milder than the summit, but late-October nights can still get close to freezing. Peg tents down properly for wind and rain, and keep a dry set of clothes for the evening no matter what the day's weather does.

If something goes wrong

Call 999 or 112, and ask for Police, then Mountain Rescue. Give your location as precisely as you can — a grid reference from your map, or a what3words address, is far more useful than "near the top."

Camp & group gear

  • Warm sleeping bag (rated for near-freezing) & sleeping mat

  • Head torches + spare batteries for camp
  • Portable power bank(s)

  • Dry bags for spare clothes
  • Bin bags — pack out everything you bring in
  • Cash/card for the campsite
  • Wellies or camp shoes for around the site

In your pack on summit day

  • Worn-in waterproof walking boots
  • Waterproof jacket and trousers
  • Warm mid-layer (fleece or softshell — no cotton)
  • Hat and gloves
  • 2+ litres of water
  • Packed lunch & high-energy snacks
  • Head torch & spare batteries, even for a day hike
  • Phone, fully charged, plus a power bank
  • Personal first aid basics & blister plasters
  • Whistle
  • Some cash
  • A bin bag for your own rubbish
